\section{Motivation}

Large-scale quantum computers are of interest in solving problems in cryptography, chemistry, and xxxxxxxx, which would otherwise be intractable for classical computers [ref?]. Trapped ion qubits are a particular promising platform to build scalable quantum computers, due to their long coherence times and high fidelity gate operations.\cite{Bruzewicz_2019} The dominant method for confining ions is through using RF-oscillatory electric fields, known as Paul traps. Implementations of Paul traps began as macroscopic "four-wire" traps made through macroscopic fabrication techniques , but can also be miniturazed into a chip form factor \cite{chiaverini2005surfaceelectrodearchitectureiontrapquantum}. These chips, called surface-electrode traps, may be fabricated with already well known microfabrication techniques, which provide a path towards scaling qubit counts. In this project, we would like to explore the design and manufacturing process of microfabricated surface electrode traps, specifically to explore designing multi-layered traps for more flexible ion shuttling in QCCD architectures, where ions are moved between trapping zones to do computation, caching, or to be stored as memory [ref?].
