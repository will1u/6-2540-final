## Where to read

- Eqs. (1)-(5) (the paper has five numbered equations): Eq. (4) the RF-null condition (field zero at each trap site); Eq. (5) curvature matching. [verify numbers]
- Figs. 1-3: Fig. 1 reference ring (κ̂ = 0.298, η̂ = 0.0196, U_rf = 50 V, Ω_rf = 2π × 200 MHz, z = 30 µm for ⁹Be⁺); Fig. 2 electrode shapes vs trap height; Fig. 3 bilayer honeycomb and principal frequencies along three orthogonal directions (ω₀ × (φ⁻¹, 1, φ)). [verify]
- Discussion: Coulomb-driven displacement of the ion from the RF null and how control electrodes fix it (subdivide the unit cell or the optimised electrodes). [verify]

## Per-source numbers

- **Tilt angle reached:** none. The text does not discuss the orientation of principal axes relative to the surface; it uses cylindrically symmetric curvature tensors in Figs. 1-2. [verify]
- **How:** the method is for a binary rf/ground electrode pattern, one RF source. Asymmetric rail widths are not the subject.
- **Voltages:** the design uses a single RF amplitude; no DC voltages are given.
- **Ion height:** z = 30 µm in the Fig. 1 context; heights z±/b = 0.4 ± 1/(2√6) in Fig. 3 (b is lattice period). [verify]
- **Frequencies:** ω̄ = κ × 2π × 53 MHz and trap depth scale 4.7 eV in the Fig. 1 context. [verify]

## Costs

- The paper says static forces from Coulomb interactions push ions off the RF null and that control electrodes can compensate; it says static fields from additional electrodes are not considered. This is the cost to keep in mind for any tilt geometry: an off-centre null needs a DC fix. [verify]
- Out-of-plane forces can arise from non-planar or insufficiently symmetric unit cells (Fig. 3 context). [verify]
