# Niedermayr et al. 2014, cryogenic silicon surface ion trap

**Citation (as found).** M. Niedermayr, K. Lakhmanskiy, M. Kumph, S. Partel, J. Edlinger, M. Brownnutt, R. Blatt, "Cryogenic silicon surface ion trap", New J. Phys. 16, 113068 (2014), DOI 10.1088/1367-2630/16/11/113068 (as printed on the arXiv abstract page). arXiv:1403.5208.

**Link.** https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.5208 (also https://arxiv.org/pdf/1403.5208 and https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1403.5208)

**Status.** Abstract page opened. The PDF fetch failed (too large for the fetch tool). The ar5iv HTML was read through a summarising fetch: section headings and figure captions are as reported there. Not checked against the printed PDF.

## Where to read

- Sec. 2 "Design overview and fabrication" and Fig. 1 (schematic and cross-section): the caption is the claimed source of the unequal RF widths. [verify]
- Sec. 3 "Cryogenic setup": the claimed source of the tilt statement (principal axes about 20° from x and y under the DC configuration used). [verify]
- Sec. 4 (silicon at cryogenic temperatures) and Sec. 5-6 (lifetime, heating): not relevant to tilt; skip unless the cryogenic loss numbers matter for us.
- Appendix A.2 (LC resonator and DC filter): relevant to the RF and DC filter question, not to tilt.

## Per-source numbers (all [verify])

- **Tilt angle reached:** about 20° of the radial principal axes from x and y under the DC voltage set used. The paper is said to say no principal axis should be perpendicular to the surface; the angle from the surface normal is not stated. [verify]
- **How:** RF electrodes of unequal widths, 200 µm and 400 µm (Fig. 1 caption), so both radial axes have an in-plane component. [verify]
- **Electrode dimensions:** centre DC 250 µm wide; segmented DC 350 µm axial, seven per side; 10 µm electrode separation. [verify]
- **Ion height:** 230 µm above the centre electrode. [verify]
- **Frequencies:** RF 20.6 MHz at 140 V amplitude; axial about 1 MHz (1.069 MHz in the heating-rate run, Fig. 4). Radial frequencies not stated numerically. [verify]

## Costs to check

- The large ion height (230 µm) makes the tilt small in absolute terms for a given DC voltage, and the tilt value here is for a cryogenic trap at a different scale from ours (50 µm). Not directly comparable. [inference]
- The summary does not state the RF-null shift or the DC voltages needed for the tilt. Look for them in Sec. 3 and the figures.
