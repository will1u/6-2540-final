# Named papers for the trap-integrated SNSPD geometry audit: Suleimen et al. PR Applied 2026 (DOI 10.1103/771x-57wg) and Clem & Berggren PRB 84, 174510 (2011)

Audited geometry, for reference: NbN meander, W = 100 nm wire, 200 nm pitch (gap g = 100 nm, fill factor f = 0.5), 150 lines x 30 um (A30) or 60 um (B60), square-cornered 180-degree turnarounds (100 nm end bar, so end-bar width W' = W), under 10 um SiO2 + 80 nm SiO2 cap, on 238 nm SiO2 over an Al mirror, Au centre DC electrode with a 30 x 30 um or 60 x 30 um window, 138Ba+ trap with RF 53 V at 28 MHz, 493 nm photons.

## Q1. What is paper 1 (10.1103/771x-57wg), and what are its main findings, design choices and quantitative results?

### Takeaway
Paper 1 is Suleimen et al., "Mitigation of rf-induced critical-current shifts in NbN superconducting nanowire single-photon detectors for trapped-ion readout", Phys. Rev. Applied 25, 044091 (30 April 2026). It deals directly with NbN SNSPDs in ion-trap RF fields. The abstract reports three things: a transparent conducting ITO shield, a nanowire-width result (300 nm wide wire keeps a higher relative I_c under stronger RF than 100 nm wide wire), and phase-coherent active RF cancellation that recovers 92% of the unperturbed I_c at 2pi x 25 MHz. I could only read the abstract page, so all device-level numbers are still missing.

### Inferences
- Paper 1 bears directly on the project: same material (NbN), same environment (RF surface trap), and it compares 100 nm against 300 nm wire width. The audited design uses 100 nm, the width the abstract reports as less RF tolerant. The team should read the Results figures comparing the two widths before fixing W. My guess at the mechanism (not confirmed from the text): a wider wire has a larger absolute I_c, so a given induced RF current is a smaller fraction of it.
- The 92% recovery number is for active cancellation at 25 MHz, close to this trap's 28 MHz. The audited design has Al-mirror shielding and the Au DC electrode overhead, but no ITO over the window. Paper 1's ITO-shield results are the comparison to look for.
- Most relevant sections for the team, judging by headings (captions not visible): Experimental Methods (sample geometry, ITO stack, how the RF is applied), Results and Discussion (I_c vs RF amplitude for 100 vs 300 nm wires; active-cancellation figure), and the Appendices (likely the field simulations). Check whether they report the ITO's optical transmission and whether the measurements used an actual trap or a model electrode structure.

## Q2. Clem & Berggren: verified identity, sharp-hairpin suppression for W = 100 nm, gap = 100 nm, optimal bend shape and recovery

### Takeaway
Verified: J. R. Clem (Ames Lab / Iowa State) and K. K. Berggren (MIT; TU Delft), "Geometry-dependent critical currents in superconducting nanocircuits", PRB 84, 174510 (2011), arXiv 1109.4881. The model is London, with I_c set where the vortex-entry barrier at the inner edge vanishes. For a sharp rectangular 180-degree turn, Eq. (100) gives R = (3/2)(pi alpha xi / 2W)^(1/3) with alpha = sqrt(1 - (W/a)^2). For W = 100 nm, gap 100 nm (f = 0.5) and xi = 4-7 nm, that is R of about 0.54-0.65. That is my application of their formula, not a number they print. The optimally rounded turnaround (Eq. 58/59, Fig. 10) gives R = 1, but only at f = 1/3 (gap = 2W). Above f = 1/3, I_c must fall even with ideal rounding.

### Cited Findings
- Identity and arXiv version: arXiv 1109.4881, authors John R. Clem and Karl K. Berggren, title as above; MIT DSpace handle 1721.1/69832. — [ar5iv 1109.4881](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1109.4881); [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/69832)
- Assumptions (Secs. II-IV): thickness d << lambda, width W << Pearl length Lambda = 2 lambda^2/d, xi << W, London vortex self-energy with a core cutoff at xi, and no current-induced suppression of the order parameter. I_c is defined as the current at which the Gibbs barrier to vortex entry at the edge goes to zero. Straight strip: K_c = phi0/(e pi mu0 xi Lambda), Eq. (14). — [ar5iv 1109.4881](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1109.4881)
- Sharp 180-degree turnaround with zero gap (slit, Sec. VII.1, Fig. 6): R = (pi xi / a)^(1/2), Eq. (56). Examples: R = 0.56 at xi = 0.1a and 0.18 at xi = 0.01a. — [ar5iv 1109.4881](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1109.4881)
- Sharp rectangular 180-degree turnaround with a finite gap (Sec. VII.5, Fig. 11). Geometry: total film width 2a; a slot of width 2h with sharp 90-degree corners is cut out for x < 0; arm width W = a - h; alpha = sqrt(1 - (W/a)^2). Eq. (100): R = (3/2)(pi alpha xi / 2W)^(1/3). Eq. (99): delta_c = e^(3/2) xi/3, about 1.49 xi. Eq. (101): barrier G_b = (3 phi0^2 / 4 pi mu0 Lambda) ln(K_c/K_I). In the W << a limit, R is about (3/2)(pi xi / 2W)^(1/3). The approximations fail when the gap 2h is of order xi. — [ar5iv 1109.4881](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1109.4881)
- Fill-factor form for meanders with sharp rectangular turns (Sec. XIII, used against Yang et al.). With f = W/p and p = W + g: I_c(f)/I_c(0) = (1 + 2f - 3f^2)^(1/6) / (1 + f)^(1/3), Eq. (175), valid up to a crossover near f of about 1 - xi/W. As f -> 1: I_c(1)/I_c(0) = (2^(4/3)/3)(pi xi/W)^(1/6), Eq. (176). Here I_c(0) is the sharp-turn I_c at vanishing fill factor. — [ar5iv 1109.4881](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1109.4881)
- Comparison with Yang et al. meanders (Sec. XIII): with xi = 7 nm and W = 90 nm they get R = 0.744. The single fit parameter I_c(0) = 17 uA was chosen by eye, against a predicted 23 uA for a long straight 90 nm strip. — [ar5iv 1109.4881](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1109.4881)
- Sharp 90-degree corner (Sec. VIII): R = (3/2)(pi xi / 4a)^(1/3), Eq. (108), with a = strip width. Examples: R of about 0.64 at xi = 0.1a and 0.30 at xi = 0.01a. The barrier prefactor is 3/2 that of the straight strip, Eq. (109). The W << a sharp 180-degree result is larger than the sharp 90-degree result by 2^(1/3) = 1.26. — [ar5iv 1109.4881](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1109.4881)
- Optimally rounded 180-degree turnaround (Sec. VII.2, Figs. 6a/10). Inner boundary x_o(y) = (a/pi) ln[2 cos(pi y/a)], Eq. (58), equivalently Eq. (59), with a = 2W. Minimum radius of curvature 2W/pi, about 0.637W. The sheet current is constant along the inner boundary, so R = 1 (no crowding) for xi << W. The cost is a gap of 2W, i.e. f = 1/3. The paper says raising f above 1/3 "must come at the expense of the critical current" (Sec. XIV). — [ar5iv 1109.4881](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1109.4881)
- Rounded turnarounds at fixed fill factor (Sec. VII.4, Figs. 8-9), London result valid for r' >> xi, so R does not depend on xi. Eqs. (79)-(81) give b - 1, R and r'/r vs f. Fig. 9 plots R, b - 1, r'/W and r'/r vs f; solid curves are end-bar width W' = W and dashed are W' = infinity. Fig. 8 examples at f = 0.5 (r'/W = 0.5): one panel gives R = 0.599 (b - 1 = 0.572), the other R = 0.707 (b - 1 = 0.250). — [ar5iv 1109.4881](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1109.4881)
- Optimal 90-degree bend (Sec. VIII.2, Eqs. 111-113): minimum radius 4W/pi, about 1.27W. Optimal narrow-to-wide pad transition (Sec. X.2): follow the S = -K_I W/4 streamline. Rounded T-junctions (Sec. IX.2). Semicircular edge notch gives R of about 1/2 (Sec. XI.1); triangular notch: Eq. (164), Table 1. — [ar5iv 1109.4881](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1109.4881)
- Thermal activation (Sec. XII): switching rate Omega = omega exp(-G_b / k_B T), Eq. (166). Most probable switching current, Eq. (173). The barrier prefactor differs by geometry (Eq. 57 for the sharp 180-degree slit, 3/2 x straight for sharp corners). Sec. XIV says dark-count frequency is increased at sharp corners because the barrier is reduced there. — [ar5iv 1109.4881](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1109.4881)
- Radius-of-curvature regime (Sec. VI): when the inner radius rho_c is comparable to or smaller than xi, the crowding reduction is partly offset, so a "sharp" corner is effectively rounded at scale xi. — [ar5iv 1109.4881](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1109.4881)

### Inferences
- Applying Eq. (100) to the audited geometry (my arithmetic): W = 100 nm, 2h = 100 nm, so h = 50 nm, a = 150 nm, W/a = 2/3, alpha = sqrt(5)/3 = 0.745. Results: R = 0.54 (xi = 4 nm), 0.58 (xi = 5 nm), 0.65 (xi = 7 nm, the value C&B used for the Yang comparison). As a cross-check, Eq. (175) at f = 0.5 gives a factor 0.907 relative to an isolated sharp hairpin, which matches alpha^(1/3) = 0.906. So the f = 0.5 penalty from the neighbour line is only about 9% on top of the sharp-corner penalty. Since R scales as xi^(1/3), the effective xi(T) at the operating temperature matters, and it is larger than xi(0).
- The rectangular-turn geometry in C&B Fig. 11 continues as a full-width film (width 2a) beyond the slot end, i.e. a wide end region. The audited design has a narrow 100 nm end bar (W' = W) with square outer corners as well. In the rounded case C&B find W' = W worse than W' = infinity (Fig. 9, solid vs dashed). So by analogy, Eq. (100) may be optimistic for a narrow end bar. That is an extrapolation; C&B do not compute this exact shape.
- At f = 0.5, even an ideally rounded (non-optimal-shape) turnaround with r' = W/2 = 50 nm is limited to R of about 0.6-0.7 (Fig. 8/9). Full recovery (R = 1) needs the Eq. (58) curve with gap = 2W (pitch 3W = 300 nm for W = 100 nm, f = 1/3), plus a widened end region. Widening the turnaround locally lets the straight-section fill factor stay above 1/3; the team should look at Sec. VII.4 and Fig. 9 to see the trade.
- For the team to read themselves: Sec. III-IV (criterion, Eqs. 12-15), Sec. VII.2 + Fig. 6a/Fig. 10 (optimal turnaround, Eqs. 58-59), Sec. VII.4 + Figs. 8-9 (rounded turns vs fill factor, Eqs. 78-81), Sec. VII.5 + Fig. 11 (sharp rectangular turn, Eqs. 96-101), Sec. VIII (90-degree corners, Eqs. 107-113, relevant to the contact-tab and lead corners), Sec. X.2 (narrow wire into wide pad, relevant to the 40 x 3 um tab joints), Sec. XII (thermal activation and dark counts), Sec. XIII (Eqs. 175-176, Yang comparison).
- Suggested sanity check (not a result): compute R with Eq. (100) using a measured or literature xi for the team's NbN at the planned operating T, and compare I_b/I_c headroom against the expected RF-induced current from paper 1 and Slichter et al.

## Q3. What did follow-up experiments measure (Hortensius 2012, Henrich 2012, Akhlaghi)?

### Takeaway
Experiments confirm that sharp corners suppress I_c and that rounding helps. The measured suppression is weaker than C&B's London prediction. NbTiN 90-degree sharp corners gave 0.51 measured vs 0.33 predicted (Hortensius). NbN 300 nm wires with sharp 90-degree bends gave about 0.6 at 4.2 K, and about 0.65 at 0.3 T_c against theory below 0.5 (Henrich). So C&B's R is a conservative (pessimistic) bound, but the direction and order of magnitude hold.

### Cited Findings
- Hortensius, Driessen, Klapwijk, Berggren, Clem, "Critical-current reduction in thin superconducting wires due to current crowding", APL 100, 182602 (2012), arXiv 1203.4253. Samples: 8 nm NbTiN on H-passivated high-resistivity Si, T_c = 9.5 K, xi of about 7 nm, Pearl length about 20 um. Wires 1 um wide, 20 um long. Sharp 90-degree corners (actual inner radius about 60 nm) gave I_c = 280-292 uA, against 382-560 uA for straight and optimally rounded wires at 4.2 K. Measured R is about 0.51 for the sharp corner (predicted 0.33) and 0.64 for a sudden-widening stub (predicted 0.46). Optimally rounded corners were indistinguishable from straight wires. Fig. 3 shows I_c histograms (1000 shots per device); Fig. 4 shows I_c(T) and the distribution width. — [ar5iv 1203.4253](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1203.4253); [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.4253)
- Henrich, Reichensperger, Hofherr, Meckbach, Il'in, Siegel (KIT), Semenov (DLR), Zotova, Vodolazov (IPM RAS), "Geometry-induced reduction of the critical current in superconducting nanowires", PRB 86, 144504 (2012), arXiv 1204.0616. Samples: 10.4 nm NbN on R-plane sapphire, T_c = 13.1-13.6 K, xi_GL(0) of about 5 nm, wires 250-300 nm wide. At 4.2 K: straight j_c = 13.3 MA/cm2; two sharp 90-degree bends gave 8.1 MA/cm2 (about 60%). Rounded 90-degree bends with inner radius r = 500 nm reached about 80% (Fig. 2). Fig. 1 covers bend angles from 0 to 90 degrees. — [ar5iv 1204.0616](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1204.0616)
- Henrich temperature dependence (Figs. 3-4). Bent and straight lines match from T_c down to about 0.7 T_c; below that the bent ones fall lower, with a bent/straight ratio of about 0.65 at 0.3 T_c. Both the London (C&B Eq. 108) and GL predictions are below 0.5 at low T; GL exceeds London by about 1.3x at w = 60 xi_GL(0). The authors conclude the reduction is "significantly less than either model predicts" and attribute this most likely to film inhomogeneities competing with the bends. A rounding radius of about 25 nm from fabrication was checked and found to be a negligible correction. — [ar5iv 1204.0616](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1204.0616)
- Henrich: straight-line I_c is about 55% of the depairing current for T < 0.9 T_c. — [ar5iv 1204.0616](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1204.0616)

### Inferences
- With a straight-line I_c of only about 55% of depairing (Henrich), the "straight" reference is already limited by defects in real NbN. Corner suppression then shows up as a smaller extra factor than the ideal theory predicts. For a 100 nm NbN wire at f = 0.5 with square turns, a realistic expectation is probably somewhere between C&B's 0.54-0.65 and roughly 0.7-0.8. That is an extrapolation from the 90-degree data, not a measurement on this geometry.
- Henrich's wires were 250-300 nm and Hortensius's 1 um. Both are wider (larger W/xi) than the audited 100 nm wire, and wider means stronger predicted crowding (R scales as (xi/W)^(1/3)). So the data are not a direct test at W = 100 nm.
