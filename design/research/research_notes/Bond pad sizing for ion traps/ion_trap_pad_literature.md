# Bond pad sizes, pitches and packaging in published surface-electrode ion traps

> **Claude-authored.** Raw research notes (literature extraction, 2026-11-08) for the bond-pad sizing report.

Scope note: every number below was read from the source text (PDF text extracted locally from the arXiv / repository copy). Where a source was found but not readable in this session (too large to fetch, or no text layer), it is listed under Gaps rather than guessed. No numbers were estimated from scale bars.

## Q1. What pad sizes, pitches, pad counts and packages were used in real traps?

### Takeaway
Only a few papers state on-die bond pad dimensions explicitly. The best-documented case is Sandia's Phoenix/Peregrine (HOA platform): 95 um wide pads with 5 um gaps (100 um pitch), 100 signal pads plus 2 RF pads, no pads on the central isthmus, in a custom 102-pin AlN CPGA/CLGA. An older MIT/Chuang-group trap used 300 x 300 um Al pads on a 1.0 cm die in a 100-pin CPGA. Most other papers (NIST SNSPD traps, MIT LL photonic traps, ETH photonic trap) state only "wirebonded to a carrier" with no pad dimensions in the text.

### Inferences
- Pitch range in the documented cases: 100 um (Sandia Phoenix/Peregrine pads; also the Zhao TSV pitch). The only explicit pad area found is 300 x 300 um (Leibrandt 2009, a 2009-era design). For comparison, the project's current 120 x 200 um pads at 180 um pitch fall between these.
- Pad counts of ~100 signals per die are set by the 100-pin CPGA standard used across Sandia, GTRI and the MIT/Chuang-group traps; a ~36-DC + 1-RF design is well under that.
- Sandia places pads on the "long ends of the bowtie", i.e. away from the beam path across the isthmus. This is a different geometry from a rectangular die with pad rows along two edges, so the Sandia pitch is not a direct template for corner keep-outs.
- Parallel-plate estimate (my calculation, not from a source; ignores fringing): a 120 x 200 um pad over 10 um SiO2 (relative permittivity ~3.9) to an Al ground plane is ~0.08 pF; a 200 x 200 um pad is ~0.14 pF. Compare with the RF-to-ground budgets in Q3 (die 5-7 pF, CPGA ~3-4 pF).

## Q2. Packaging: carriers, interposers, bond wire lengths, filters

### Takeaway
The common pattern is a 100-pin CPGA, often with a ceramic spacer or interposer that raises the trap surface above the package for side laser access. Sandia moved to a custom AlN bowtie CPGA/CLGA with on-die trench capacitors. GTRI and A*STAR used TSVs plus an interposer so the bond wires sit on the interposer, not the trap. Filter capacitors are on the die (trench capacitors), on the interposer, on the carrier perimeter, or outside vacuum.

### Cited Findings
- Sandia Phoenix/Peregrine package: HTCC AlN chosen over alumina for higher thermal conductivity and a closer thermal-expansion match to Si; package routing is 10 um W; surface plated with at least 2.5 um Au on at least 1.27 um Ni; RF trace routed on the outside of the package with parallel vias to cut resistance; all exposed non-signal metal is grounded; package RF trace 40.8 mOhm and 3.7 pF, updated package 2.9 pF — [arXiv:2009.02398, Sec. 5.2 and Sec. 6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.02398)
- Sandia die attach: Au80Sn20 solder (jetted, reflowed in N2 or formic acid), SAC305 solder, or EpoTek H21D epoxy cured at 225 C — [arXiv:2009.02398, Sec. 5.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.02398)
- Sandia on-die filtering: 146 trench capacitors of 0.8 nF each; electrodes with more capacitance to RF get more than one — [arXiv:2009.02398, Sec. 6.2 netlist text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.02398)
- Stick et al.: die attached with conductive cyanate ester adhesive (JM7000) to a 1.5 mm ceramic spacer; control electrodes capacitively grounded outside the vacuum chamber; low-pass filters of 3 kHz or 200 kHz — [arXiv:1008.0990](https://arxiv.org/abs/1008.0990)
- Guise et al.: "Capacitors on the surface of the chip or bonded at the perimeter of the carrier are used to filter out radio frequency (RF) pickup"; with the BGA design, "Filter capacitors located at the edge of the CPGA would be less effective in this architecture, due to distance from the trap"; trench capacitors measured 103.5 +/- 1.2 pF on die and 118 +/- 3 pF packaged, breakdown ~18 V; trench capacitors cut die area by a factor of thirty compared with planar capacitors — [arXiv:1412.5576, Sec. I and Sec. II B](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.5576)
- Guise et al. interposer: four Au metal layers (1.7 um each) with 4, 6 and 3 um SiO2 between; M1 and M4 are ground planes; perimeter openings on M3 are for wire-bonding to the package, central openings for solder-bonding to the trap die — [arXiv:1412.5576, Appendix (interposer process steps 1-7)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.5576)
- Leibrandt et al.: spacer needed "to elevate the chip above the upper surface of the carrier for laser access across the top of the trap chip"; solder (80/20 Au/Sn) attach worked best; epoxy and ceramic paste could not be cleaned after mounting, and ceramic paste sometimes let the chip detach during wire bonding; in the cryogenic setup "The RF lead is not routed through the CPGA to reduce capacitance" — [arXiv:0904.2599, packaging section and Fig. 8 caption](https://arxiv.org/abs/0904.2599)
- Sage et al. (cryogenic, MIT LL): signals routed lead connector -> filter boards (single-stage low-pass) -> alumina interposer -> chip, with Au wire bonds at each junction; cooling of the trap mount "is achieved primarily through the ~100 Au wire bonds"; chip reached 4.3 K with all wires connected; RF power (~5 mW) caused insignificant heating — [arXiv:1205.6379, Fig. 1 caption and apparatus section](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.6379)
- Spivey et al. (cryogenic, 5 K): HOA 2.0 interposer contains trench capacitors to filter RF on the DC electrodes; the CPGA is pressed into a 100-pin socket on a PCB — [arXiv:2108.05290, Sec. 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05290)
- Romaszko et al. review: describes a bare-die SWTF1 12-channel RC filter array (35 kOhm, 220 pF) mounted on a PCB with silver epoxy and wirebonded; discusses wafer-scale stud bonding problems with high stress — [Romaszko et al., Engineering of microfabricated ion traps and integration of advanced on-chip features, Nat. Rev. Phys. 2, 285 (2020), arXiv:1908.00267](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.00267)
- Hughes et al. review: on-chip meander-line resistors as part of the DC filters; "Bringing filters closer to the electrodes can reduce the noise induced in connecting wires as the filters are typically located outside the vacuum system or on the chip carrier" — [Hughes et al., Microfabricated Ion Traps, Contemp. Phys. (2011), arXiv:1101.3207](https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.3207)
- Bond wire lengths: Guise studs ~75 um base to tip; Tabakov loops protrude at most 30 um above the electrode plane; Stick ribbons pulled taut. No paper read here gives a full trap-to-package wire span in mm — [arXiv:1412.5576](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.5576); [arXiv:1501.06554](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.06554); [arXiv:1008.0990](https://arxiv.org/abs/1008.0990)

### Inferences
- In the cryogenic setups (Sage 2012, Spivey 2021) wire bonds and interposers carry heat as well as signals, so bond count and wire material affect the chip's base temperature. This matters for an SNSPD-equipped die.
- Pins per edge: Phoenix uses 100 package pads around a bowtie; no paper states pads per die edge directly.

### Gaps
- No source gave a CPGA cavity size or a trap-pad-to-package-pad distance in mm.
- No ion-trap paper read here specifies wire diameter for signal bonds, except the Haltli et al. study (25 um Au, see Q4) and the Stick ribbons.

## Q3. Why pad size and placement matter (as stated in the literature)

### Takeaway
The literature cites four reasons. (1) Pads and their leads add RF capacitance to ground, and dissipation scales with capacitance squared. (2) Pads and filter capacitors use up die area and perimeter. (3) Bond wires and pads in the beam path scatter laser light and block optical access. (4) Pads should sit on or above a grounded layer that shields the lossy Si. The usual responses are pads only at the die ends, low-profile or taut bonds, pads on a buried metal level, and in newer designs TSV/BGA plus an interposer.

### Cited Findings
- RF capacitance budget (Phoenix, pre-bake test): packaged device should be 11 pF, of which 7 +/- 1 pF is the trap die (5.5 +/- 1 pF for Peregrine) and 4 +/- 1 pF the CPGA carrier; the feedthrough and socket add ~6 pF — [arXiv:2009.02398, Sec. 8.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.02398)
- RF dissipation (Phoenix Table 1, package excluded): Peregrine 5.4 pF / 0.36 Ohm / 20 mW; Phoenix 7.1 pF / 0.4 Ohm / 35 mW; HOA-2.1 7.6 pF / 0.9 Ohm / 100 mW. Ps is approximately (1/2) Rs U^2 omega^2 Cp^2. "The resistive loss of the rf dominates over the absorptive loss from the dielectric." The RF electrode was bonded with 7 wire bonds "directly to the package to keep the capacitance as low as possible" — [arXiv:2009.02398, Sec. 4.4, Table 1, Fig. 24 caption](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.02398)
- Stick et al.: total RF-to-ground capacitance 7 pF (trap and CPGA package), measured with 30 MHz, 2 W drive through a Q = 150 resonator; the lower plane M1 "serves primarily as a ground plane to prevent RF coupling into the lossy silicon substrate" — [arXiv:1008.0990](https://arxiv.org/abs/1008.0990)
- Tabakov et al.: M1 "contains I/O bond pads as well as an rf ground plane to electrically shield the rf leads of the trap structure from the bulk (lossy) Si substrate"; total RF capacitance to ground 7 pF — [arXiv:1501.06554](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.06554)
- Zhao et al.: removing the wire-bond pads and connecting circuits from the trap die cut the single-RF-electrode area from 4.5 to 0.2 mm^2 and the RF-GND capacitance from 32 pF (wire-bonded trap, Si, no ground plane) to 3 pF, with "two orders of magnitude RF loss reduction"; "power loss is proportional to the square of parasitic capacitance" — [arXiv:2101.00869, p. 2-3, Fig. 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00869)
- Guise et al.: "the capacitors and bond pads can consume a majority of the overall trap chip area and perimeter, strongly constraining the layout of electrode structures and DC/RF lead traces. Wirebonds must be carefully arranged to minimize obstructions to laser access"; novel traps "are still constrained by wirebonds along two edges"; in their BGA design the 10 um SiO2 between M1 and M2 "limits the capacitance of the RF electrodes"; with bonds on the interposer "the surface of the BGA trap chip remains unobstructed for laser access" — [arXiv:1412.5576, Sec. I and Sec. II B](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.5576)
- Guise et al.: an 8 um-waist beam begins to clip on the CPGA carrier edge, losing a few percent of the beam — [arXiv:1412.5576, Sec. III (Fig. 17 discussion)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.5576)
- Sandia Phoenix: "Wirebonding locations are optimized for beam access"; "The trap center is kept clear of wirebonds to enable laser beams to be introduced along the linear axis of the trap"; the package "was optimized to not further impede 370 nm light perpendicular to the isthmus" — [arXiv:2009.02398, Sec. 4.1, Sec. 5.3, Fig. 4 caption](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.02398)
- Tabakov et al.: pads at the perimeter and minimized loop heights "to reduce scattering of the lasers from the wirebonds and allow unobstructed optical access across the chip surface" — [arXiv:1501.06554](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.06554)
- Romaszko et al. review: "If the wirebonds are in the path of a laser, unwanted scattering will occur, which will drastically affect the ability to perform experiments"; a design requirement is that "wirebonds do not impede the optical path" — [arXiv:1908.00267, Sec. on optical access and design-requirements list](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.00267)
- Zhao et al.: "large number of wires will further impede laser beams"; with bond wires on the interposer, the height difference "may mitigate laser beam blocking issues" — [arXiv:2101.00869, p. 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00869)
- Hughes et al. review: total resistance and capacitance of the trap lower the resonator Q, so "It is important to minimize the resistance and capacitance of the ion trap array"; dielectric loss modelled as G = omega C tan(delta); RF losses "can result in heating and destruction of trap structures" — [arXiv:1101.3207, Sec. II and Sec. V B](https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.3207)
- Stray charge on exposed dielectric: Stick et al. design around "minimizing the line of sight access to the ion from exposed dielectrics"; Guise's re-entrant metallization "screens any trapped charge"; Zhao patterned SiO2 to the electrode geometry "to avoid undesired dielectric charging" — [arXiv:1008.0990](https://arxiv.org/abs/1008.0990); [arXiv:1412.5576](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.5576); [arXiv:2101.00869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00869)
- SNSPD-specific (NIST): SNSPD leads were made "much narrower than typical SNSPD leads, a design choice made to minimize capacitive coupling to the trap rf electrodes", which required an extra 350 nm Au on the leads to avoid latching — [arXiv:2008.00065, Supplement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.00065)
- SNSPD-specific (NIST, Hampel 2023): leads routed several hundred um away from the RF electrode; grounded mirrors under the nanowire improved RF tolerance; the authors attribute residual RF sensitivity to on-chip RF power dissipation heating the SNSPD by roughly 0.5 K at 45 V peak RF (with 350 nm-thick RF electrodes in those test devices, versus 6 um in ion-trapping devices) — [Hampel et al., Trap-Integrated SNSPDs with Improved RF Tolerance, arXiv:2302.01462](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01462)

### Inferences
- Across the documented traps, the die-level RF budget is ~5-7 pF and the CPGA adds ~3-4 pF. A single pad of ~0.1 pF is small next to that, but ~36 DC pads plus their leads, each coupled to RF through the trench or filter capacitors and through lead overlap, is the quantity the literature actually tracks.
- For an SNSPD die, on-chip RF dissipation is a heating concern as well as a resonator-Q concern (Hampel 2023), which ties RF pad/lead capacitance to detector operating margin.

### Gaps
- No paper read here quantifies laser scatter as a function of pad or bond distance from the beam, or gives a minimum keep-out distance in um.
- No paper read here measures DC-pad-to-RF crosstalk attributable to the pads themselves (as opposed to leads).
- Search results listed [Design and fabrication of ion traps for low RF power dissipation (OSTI 3391614)](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/3391614), [Multi-junction surface ion trap for quantum computing (arXiv:2403.00208)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00208) and [Test and characterization of multilayer ion traps on fused silica (arXiv:2505.21284)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21284) as discussing bond-pad contributions to RF capacitance; these were not opened, so their numbers are not reported here.

## Q4. Reported failures attributable to pads or bonds

### Takeaway
Two pad/bond failure modes are documented. (1) Electrode shorts through a thin, stressed Si3N4 insulator beneath the bond pads (Leibrandt 2009). (2) Au wire on Al pad intermetallic growth during bakes, causing high-resistance bonds, heel breaks and bond lift-off (Sandia; Haltli et al. 2024). No source read here reported bond lift-off caused by cooling to 4 K, or RF breakdown at a pad.

### Cited Findings
- Leibrandt et al.: some traps "developed shorts between electrodes either during vacuum bakeout or after trapping ions for a few weeks. The most likely cause ... is the thin silicon nitride insulating layer between the electrodes and the ground plane or Si substrate. This layer is especially stressed beneath the wire bonding pads"; they propose SiO2 instead of nitride; Innsbruck "were not able to trap ions due to shorting"; the failure-mode table lists "electrical shorts" for several test groups — [arXiv:0904.2599, Sec. on results/discussion and summary table](https://arxiv.org/abs/0904.2599)
- Haltli et al. (Sandia): device bond pads are Al (50 nm TiN under 1.2 um Al-0.5wt%Cu), package pads are Au; 25 um (1 mil) Au wire, thermosonic wedge bonding ("lower loop heights, finer pitch, and higher reliability" than ball bonds); IMC growth causes high-resistance bonds, wire breaks, Kirkendall voids and lift-off leaving a crater; heel breaks are typical for ion traps after long or repeated bakes; bare Al time to failure 384 +/- 58 h at 200 C; Ti 20 nm / Pt 100 nm / Au 250 nm cap extends this to ~2100 +/- 400 h (extrapolated) — [Haltli, Ou, Nordquist, Clark, Revelle, Mitigating the Effects of Au-Al Intermetallic Compounds Due to High-Temperature Processing of Surface Electrode Ion Traps, arXiv:2402.12546, Sec. I-III, Table I](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12546)
- Sandia Phoenix manual: recommends a maximum bake of 200 C for less than 7 days to "mitigate the formation of brittle and high resistivity gold-aluminum inter-metallic at the wire bonds" — [arXiv:2009.02398, Sec. 5.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.02398)
- Sandia Phoenix manual: voltages above +/-30 V (including static electricity) will damage the trench capacitors on all control electrodes; post-packaging tests include shorts between every electrode pair, capacitance to ground per electrode, and SEM charge-contrast imaging to find disconnected wire bonds or vias; RF-to-ground resistance must exceed 40 MOhm — [arXiv:2009.02398, Sec. 5.4 and Sec. 8](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.02398)
- Stick et al.: each trap tested for shorts between all electrodes including the ground plane; no shorts below 100 MOhm observed — [arXiv:1008.0990](https://arxiv.org/abs/1008.0990)
- Hughes et al. review: breakdown happens through the bulk dielectric, across the vacuum gap, or by surface flashover; surface flashover field is measured at about a factor 2.5 below bulk breakdown for the same material; avoid sharp corners — [arXiv:1101.3207, Sec. V A](https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.3207)
- Tabakov et al.: several electrodes in the Sandia ring trap were shorted (e22, e67, e89), and nearby trap locations were excluded from measurements; the paper does not attribute these shorts to pads — [arXiv:1501.06554, Fig. caption and Sec. on field compensation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.06554)

### Inferences
- The Leibrandt case is a direct precedent for the dielectric under bond pads being the weak point: bonding force lands on the insulator between pad and ground plane. In the project's stack (Au over 10 um SiO2 over Al) the same pad-over-ground-plane geometry exists. The insulator material differs (SiO2, which Leibrandt et al. recommend over Si3N4).
- The Au-Al IMC concern applies wherever Au wire lands on Al pads. With Au electrodes and Au pads on the die, the Au-Al interface would only appear where Au meets the Al ground plane or an Al-pad carrier. This is an inference from Haltli et al., not something they tested for this stack.

### Gaps
- No source read here reports wire-bond lift-off or cracking caused by cooling to 4 K. The cryogenic papers (Sage 2012, Spivey 2021, Niffenegger 2020, Mehta 2020) report no bond failures, but none of them discusses cryogenic bond reliability explicitly.
- No source read here reports RF breakdown at a bond pad specifically.
