# Romaszko et al., microfabricated ion trap engineering (technical review)

**Citation.** Zak David Romaszko, Seokjun Hong, Martin Siegele, Reuben Kahan Puddy, Foni Raphaël Lebrun-Gallagher, Sebastian Weidt, Winfried Karl Hensinger, "Engineering of Microfabricated Ion Traps and Integration of Advanced On-Chip Features", arXiv:1908.00267. The PDF shows no journal line; the arXiv abstract page gives the related DOI 10.1038/s42254-020-0182-8 [verify].

- Box 1 p.2: ion trapping basics, intrinsic and extrinsic micromotion. Eq. (3) is the Mathieu equation.
- Sec. 2.1 p.3: five-wire geometry. Eqs. (1)-(2) give trap depth and ion height for a gapless five-wire trap (rail widths a and b). The review notes that a rail-width ratio of b/a = 3.68 maximises depth in a cited analysis [verify].
- Box 2 p.4: "the laser is typically positioned, parallel to the surface, at a 45° angle with respect to x and y". Also: "The z motion can be effectively cooled by rotating the principal axis".
- Sec. 2.2 "Rotation of principle axes" p.5 (sic, in the source): the allowed laser paths are "limited to the directions parallel or perpendicular" to the surface. Two methods are named: RF rails of different widths, and asymmetric DC voltages on rotation electrodes (a six-wire geometry). The DC method "could easily achieve an 'out-of-plane' principal axis angle of rotation of θ = 45°" [verify the exact wording].
- Fig. 2.1 p.5: caption of the six-wire rotation method and the superposition of the RF pseudopotential (a) and the DC rotation potential (b).
- The 45° figure is a statement in a review, quoted from the authors. It is not a measured value in this paper.
