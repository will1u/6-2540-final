# Allcock et al. 2010, symmetric surface trap with micromotion compensation

**Citation.** D. T. C. Allcock, J. A. Sherman, D. N. Stacey, A. H. Burrell, M. J. Curtis, G. Imreh, N. M. Linke, D. J. Szwer, S. C. Webster, A. M. Steane, D. M. Lucas, "Implementation of a symmetric surface electrode ion trap with field compensation using a modulated Raman effect", New J. Phys. 12 (2010) 053026, arXiv:0909.3272 [quant-ph]. Journal reference from the arXiv abstract page [verify against the journal].

- Sec. I.A p.1 (Geometry): the central axial electrode is split so that the radial modes rotate and couple to a beam parallel to the surface. The text contrasts this with asymmetric designs, which rotate the modes in a different way.
- Sec. I.B p.2 (Operating Parameters): the "tilt" potential is written in the basis x' = (x+y)/√2, y' = (x−y)/√2. Quote: "the tilt angle would always be 45°" (the degeneracy-lifting statement that follows says end effects modify it).
- Sec. I.B p.2: "The chosen tilt voltages break the radial mode degeneracy without overly weakening either of them" (checked against the PDF, p. 2).
- Sec. I.B p.2: an estimated tilt angle of 42° at the typical RF voltage (secular frequencies 2.29 and 3.33 MHz) (checked against the PDF, p. 2).
- Table II p. 3 gives the electrode voltage bases (mV) for the tilt term (page checked against the PDF).
- Table III p.5: measured and modelled radial frequencies against a tilt factor, with θ_model from 0° to 44°; 42° at 2.90 / 3.34 MHz measured (checked against the PDF, p. 5).
- Sec. IV p.6 (Micromotion Compensation), Eq. (4): excess micromotion amplitude and radial displacement.
- Sec. IV p.6: "Doppler cooling beams must lie in the (x̂ − ẑ) plane"; "Full radial micromotion compensation cannot be carried out in this geometry using the standard correlation technique." This is the paper's answer to the vertical micromotion question. Fig. 9 p.6 shows the repumper co-linear with the Doppler beam; the angle labelled 51° in Fig. 9(a) [verify what it is measured from].
- Sec. IV.A p.6-7 (Modified detection scheme): the repumper's Doppler shift modulates the P1/2 population through a coherent Raman process (the "modulated Raman effect" of the title). Eq. (5) and Fig. 10 p.7.
- Sec. IV.A p.7: "coarsely compensate the out-of-plane micromotion by looking at profiles" (fluorescence versus repumper detuning, Fig. 11 p.7).
- Sec. IV.B p.7: the ŷ-direction sensitivity figure of 0.9(1)% per V/m [verify].
- Fig. 13 p.8, Eq. (10): a fitted effective linewidth Γ = 25.5 MHz for the Ca+ 397 nm transition in this trap (not Ba+) [verify].
